FAQ
The useful questions. Including a few you may not know you have yet.

      
Getting started
The part where everything is still theoretically simple.
    
        
  1. What does CopySelect actually do?
    When Automatic copying is on, selecting text can put it on your clipboard without Ctrl+C. Confirmation, History, formatting, and organization are separate, so you can use as much or as little of CopySelect as you want.
    2. 
        
  2. Do I have to organize anything before using it?
    No. CopySelect is perfectly happy being useful before you’ve organized your life. CopySelect starts useful immediately. The more you organize, the better WordWatch understands how you organize.
    3. 
        
  3. Why the delay? Isn’t the point to be fast?
    Usually, yes. Local processing generally needs only about 100–150 ms. A longer Copy Delay is mostly there for the half-second in which you realize you selected three words too many. Open Automatic copying.
    4. 
        
  4. Why can keyboard selection behave differently in an editable field?
    Because Shift-selection often means “I’m about to replace this,” not “please copy this.” CopySelect waits for Shift to be released and gives editable fields a little more grace.
    5. 
        
  5. Why didn’t CopySelect copy something I selected?
    Check the master switch, the current Site Rule, minimum-selection settings, modifier requirements, and editable-field safeguards. A selection that disappears or changes before the delay finishes is also deliberately ignored.
    6. 
        
  6. I returned to a page and the old selection was still highlighted. Why didn’t it copy again?
    Because it already happened. CopySelect has been instructed not to mistake déjà vu for productivity. Only a genuinely new or changed selection should trigger another automatic copy.
    7. 
      

      
Copying, formatting & templates
What actually lands on the clipboard, and why.
    
        
  1. What is the difference between Plain Text and Rich Text?
    Plain Text keeps the words. Rich Text can preserve compatible formatting such as emphasis, links, lists, and structure from the source.
    2. 
        
  2. What is a template for?
    It’s the recipe for what lands on your clipboard. {text} is the main ingredient; title, URL, date, time, counts, and your own wording are garnish, provenance, or paperwork. Open Metadata & Templates.
    3. 
        
  3. If my template contains only {text}, will it add anything else?
    Not because of the template. Appended metadata is controlled separately. A {text}-only template should contribute only the selected text when that template is the active copy format.
    4. 
        
  4. What are “Chars (spaces)” and “Chars (no spaces)”?
    The first counts every character, including whitespace. The second ignores whitespace. Useful for strict limits and for discovering that apparently innocent spaces have been living rent-free in your count.
    5. 
        
  5. Does changing the copy format change what is stored in History?
    Not necessarily. Clipboard output and History storage are separate. You can preserve richer source information in History while copying a simpler version.
    6. 
        
  6. What does “Copy as” do in History?
    It takes an existing clip and puts it back on the clipboard in another supported format, such as Plain Text, Rich Text, a reference format, or your custom template when the required source data exists.
    7. 
        
  7. What does Ctrl+Shift+V do?
    For a CopySelect-produced clipboard entry, it can paste the original selected text without CopySelect’s surrounding metadata or rich formatting. Clipboard content from elsewhere is left alone.
    8. 
      

      
Clipboard History
Where past copying decisions come to live.
    
        
  1. What does the Metadata switch do?
    It controls whether History shows source and clip details such as domain, exact time, counts, and related information. It changes the display, not the clip itself.
    2. 
        
  2. Why do some snippets have Expand/Compress controls and others do not?
    Those controls appear only when the snippet content has something to expand. Metadata does not make a short clip expandable simply by existing nearby.
    3. 
        
  3. Why are some timeline times missing?
    They aren’t missing. CopySelect just declines to say ~1 hr ago eight times in a row. The timeline marks the first clip in a useful time group and becomes more granular only when a section gets crowded.
    4. 
        
  4. How does the timeline decide what to show?
    Very recent clips can use minute labels. Then it settles into 15-minute and hour-scale groups. Dense hour groups can split into 15-minute intervals. Older clips use Today, Yesterday, or the date with a quarter-hour time beneath it.
    5. 
        
  5. Can I see the exact time?
    Yes. The exact timestamp stays with the clip’s metadata. The timeline is for orientation rather than repeating a stopwatch beside every item.
    6. 
        
  6. What happens when History gets enormous?
    CopySelect loads it in batches rather than dramatically dumping your entire clipboard biography onto the page at once. More entries load as you approach the end.
    7. 
        
  7. What does Pin do?
    It tells CopySelect, “No, this one actually matters.” Pinned clips can be protected from routine automatic cleanup.
    8. 
        
  8. What does “Select unorganized” mean?
    It selects visible clips that have not yet acquired useful organization, so you can review them together rather than hunt individually.
    9. 
        
  9. Can I keep automatic copying but stop saving History?
    Yes. Automatic copying and local History are independent. Open Clipboard Storage Settings.
    10. 
      

      
Privacy, storage & backups
Mostly local. Deliberately uneventful.
    
        
  1. Where is my History stored?
    Locally in the browser extension’s storage on this device. Normal CopySelect operation does not require sending your clipboard History to an analytics server.
    2. 
        
  2. What does automatic cleanup remove?
    Items that meet your retention rules, with protections such as minimum keep and pinned-item handling applied according to your settings.
    3. 
        
  3. What happens to pinned items during cleanup?
    They stay pinned. Automatic cleanup is aimed at older, unpinned items.
    4. 
        
  4. Can I back up my History?
    Manual export is available in Data & Backup. Automatic rotating local History backups are planned separately. Open Data & Backup.
    5. 
        
  5. Why keep source information at all?
    When enabled, it lets you tell where something came from later instead of staring at a paragraph and hoping for a revelation.
    6. 
      

      
Tags, Collections & Saved Views
Three ways of organizing things. Similar enough to require this section.
    
        
  1. What is the difference between a tag, a Collection, and a Saved View?
    A tag describes a clip. A Collection says the clip belongs to something. A Saved View is a reusable search that finds clips meeting conditions. They overlap in usefulness, but they are not the same thing.
    2. 
        
  2. Can a clip have several tags?
    Yes.
    3. 
        
  3. Can a clip belong to more than one Collection?
    Yes. CopySelect does not believe every object must choose one drawer and live there forever.
    4. 
        
  4. Does a clip need tags before it can belong to a Collection?
    No. Collection membership is separate from ordinary tags.
    5. 
        
  5. If I remove a tag, does the clip leave its Collection?
    No. Changing a tag does not silently alter Collection membership.
    6. 
        
  6. What exactly is a Saved View?
    A search you liked enough to keep. It does not move or duplicate anything; it just knows where to look.
    7. 
        
  7. Why did a Saved View change by itself?
    Because the data it searches changed. Saved Views are live queries, not frozen folders.
    8. 
      

      
WordWatch
Rules, automation, and the gradual delegation of minor decisions.
    
        
  1. What exactly is WordWatch watching?
    Your new clips, against rules you’ve given it. Not you. That would be a different product.
    2. 
        
  2. What is the difference between Quick Setup and WordWatch Studio?
    Quick Setup handles common matching without much ceremony. Studio exposes the full rule structure, exclusions, concepts, testing, and actions.
    3. 
        
  3. Why “Any of these”?
    Because one is enough. Terms inside that group use OR logic. If any one matches, the group passes.
    4. 
        
  4. Where does AND come in?
    An AND any of these group adds another condition that must also match. OR happens inside each group; AND connects the groups.
    5. 
        
  5. What does “When matched” mean?
    Those are the actions WordWatch performs after the rule succeeds, such as adding the clip to the Collection, pinning it, adding tags, or putting it in Paste Stack.
    6. 
        
  6. Are Suggested tags real tags?
    Not yet. WordWatch has an opinion. You still get a vote.
    7. 
        
  7. What happens when I change tags?
    Relevant organization is recomputed in the background. The manual Refresh action is there when you want CopySelect to reconsider its organizational decisions from scratch. Occasionally healthy for software too. Open Collections.
    8. 
        
  8. What is a Locked Collection?
    A Collection whose clips stay out of ordinary History, search, previews, and exports until you unlock it.
    9. 
      

      
Site Rules & patterns
Because sometimes one website needs special treatment.
    
        
  1. What does “Inherit global” mean?
    This Site Rule does not override that setting. CopySelect uses your normal global value instead.
    2. 
        
  2. What does * mean?
    A wildcard. For example, *.example.com can cover subdomains such as docs.example.com and shop.example.com.
    3. 
        
  3. What does ^ mean?
    At the beginning of a pattern, it tells CopySelect you are using a regular expression rather than a simple wildcard pattern.
    4. 
        
  4. What does \ do?
    In a regular expression, it escapes characters that would otherwise have special meaning. \. means an actual dot; \\ represents an actual backslash.
    5. 
        
  5. When do I need regex?
    When * has stopped being cooperative. If a normal domain pattern works, take the win and leave regex undisturbed. Open Site Rules.
    6. 
        
  6. Which Site Rule wins if several could match?
    Rules are evaluated according to their configured priority/order. Put more precise overrides where they can take precedence over broader rules.
    7. 
      

      
Troubleshooting & reliability
Things that were not part of the plan.
    
        
  1. Why does an open page sometimes need refreshing after an update?
    Because that tab is still living in the past. Refresh it and the page and extension get the same content-script instructions again.
    2. 
        
  2. Why does Copy Confirmation happen only after the clipboard write?
    Because “Copied” should mean copied. CopySelect waits for a successful clipboard write before congratulating itself.
    3. 
        
  3. Something still looks stale. What should I try first?
    For organization, use Refresh/Recompute. After an extension update, refresh the affected webpage. If the problem is site-specific, check the matching Site Rule before dismantling the rest of your setup.
    4.
